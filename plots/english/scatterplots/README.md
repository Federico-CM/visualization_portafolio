This animation traces the relationship between fertility and life expectancy over time.
In the 1960s, countries cluster into two clearly separated groups—often labeled “first world” and “third world”—shown here in teal and orange. Over the decades, these clusters begin to overlap, revealing a convergence in health outcomes as life expectancy rises and fertility declines across much of the world.

Mexico, highlighted in black, illustrates this transition particularly well: it moves steadily away from the high-fertility, low-life-expectancy pattern toward the global middle. While health gaps have narrowed, inequality persists in dimensions beyond these two indicators.
